Posting strategy · 6 min read

How often should you
post on TikTok?

What a study of 11.4 million TikTok posts found, what TikTok itself says, and how to choose a posting rhythm you can actually keep.

By the Creator Signals team · Published 17 November 2026 · Platform facts checked 17 November 2026

The short answer

For most new creators, two to five posts a week is a good place to start. A Buffer study of 11.4 million TikTok posts found that going from one post a week to two to five gave the biggest step up in views per post (up to 17% more); posting more kept helping, but by less each step. The best schedule is the one you can keep for months without your videos getting worse.

What you'll learn

  • What the largest public study on TikTok posting frequency found
  • What TikTok itself does — and doesn't — say
  • How to pick, plan and review your own rhythm

What the data says

The most detailed public study we found comes from Buffer, a social media scheduling company. It analysed 11.4 million TikTok posts from more than 150,000 accounts and compared each account with itself in weeks when it posted more or less often. Compared with posting once a week, Buffer reports:

Posts per weekViews per post (vs. 1 a week)
2–5up to 17% more
6–10up to 29% more
11 or moreup to 34% more

Two details are easy to miss:

  • The biggest jump comes first. Going from one post to two–five a week gave the largest gain for the effort; after that, each extra post added less.
  • The typical video didn't change much. As reported by Search Engine Journal, median views stayed around 500 per post at every frequency. Posting more mostly gives you more chances at a video that does unusually well — it doesn't make every video bigger.

Keep in mind that this is one company's data from accounts that use its tool, and averages say little about your account. Use it as a sensible starting point, not a rule.

What does TikTok say?

You'll often read that “TikTok recommends posting 1–4 times a day”. That figure is widely attributed to TikTok's earlier creator guidance, but we couldn't find it in TikTok's current official help pages, so treat it with care. What TikTok has explained is that its recommendations look at how people respond to each video, and that follower count and past hits are not direct factors. In other words: each video gets judged on its own — which is why quality per video still matters more than volume.

How to choose your rhythm

  1. Start with what you can sustain. Three good videos a week for three months beats three a day for one week.
  2. Count the hours, not just the posts. Planning, filming and editing one video might take you an hour or more at first. Multiply by your target and check it fits your week.
  3. Batch. Plan ideas on one day, film several videos in one session, edit and schedule later. This is how most people keep a steady rhythm.
  4. Keep quality steady. If adding a fourth video a week makes all four worse, go back to three.
  5. Increase slowly. After a month, add one video a week if it still feels manageable.

Example weekly plans

Your timeRhythmExample

8+ hours a week5 or moreWeekdays, with two filming sessions a week

Review every two weeks

Open TikTok Studio → Analytics and compare your videos by average watch time, retention and shares and saves, not just views. If your numbers improve and the workload feels fine, keep going or add one video a week. If quality slips, scale back. Change one thing at a time so you know what made the difference.

📝 Exercise

Write down how many hours you can realistically spend on TikTok each week. Divide by the time one video takes you, round down, and plan the next two weeks on a calendar — including one batch-filming session.

Key takeaways

  • Two to five posts a week is a sensible start for most new creators.
  • In Buffer's study the biggest gain came from moving beyond one post a week; extra posts added less each time.
  • Median views barely changed with frequency — more posts mainly mean more chances.
  • The “1–4 times a day” advice isn't in TikTok's current official help pages we checked.
  • Choose a rhythm you can keep, batch your work, and review every two weeks.
