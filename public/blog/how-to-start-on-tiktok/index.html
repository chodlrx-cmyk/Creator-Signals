How to start on TikTok:
a beginner's plan.

A practical, step-by-step plan for your first month on TikTok — from choosing a niche and setting up your profile to your first videos and reading your own numbers.

By the Creator Signals team · Published 17 November 2026 · Platform facts checked 17 November 2026

The short answer

Pick one topic and a clear viewer, set up a profile that says what people get from you, study your niche for a few days, then publish a small batch of simple videos on a schedule you can keep — two to five a week is a good start. After about seven videos, look at your analytics and change one thing at a time.

What you'll learn

  • How TikTok decides who sees your videos — in TikTok's own words
  • The seven steps from “I want to start” to your first published videos
  • What to measure after your first week, and what to ignore

Step 1 — Understand how people find your videos

Most viewers will meet you on the For You feed, not on your profile. TikTok has explained that this feed ranks videos for each person based on a mix of signals: how they interact (videos they like, share, comment on or create, accounts they follow), information about the video (such as captions, sounds and hashtags), and device and account settings such as language and country — the last group carrying less weight.

Two details from TikTok's explanation matter most for beginners:

  • Watching counts. TikTok gives more weight to strong signals, such as someone watching a longer video from beginning to end, than to weak ones.
  • You don't need followers first. TikTok says follower count and previous high-performing videos are not direct factors in its recommendations. Accounts with more followers may get more views because they have a bigger audience — but every new video gets its own chance.

In practice: make each video worth watching to the end for one specific viewer. That matters more than any trick.

Step 2 — Choose a niche and one clear viewer

A niche is simply a topic you can make many videos about, for a viewer you can picture. Write one sentence: “I help [who] with [what], so they can [result].” For example: “I help busy students cook cheap dinners in 15 minutes.”

Then pick three content pillars — recurring themes inside your niche (for the example above: budget recipes, kitchen basics, meal-prep routines). Pillars stop you from running out of ideas and make your profile easy to understand.

Step 3 — Set up your account and profile

  • Age: in most of the world you must be at least 13 to have a TikTok account. Some features have higher age limits — for example, TikTok's Community Guidelines say content from people under 16 isn't eligible for the For You feed.
  • Username and name: short, easy to say, and ideally the same as on other platforms.
  • Profile photo: a clear face or logo that is still recognisable when small.
  • Bio: say what viewers get — use your one-sentence niche from Step 2.

Step 4 — Study your niche for a few days

Spend 15–20 minutes a day watching videos in your topic. Save the ones that kept you watching and note why: the first line, the pacing, the on-screen text, the ending. You're not copying — you're learning what your future viewers already enjoy.

Step 5 — Make your first three videos

Keep them simple. One video, one idea. A useful structure is:

  1. Hook (first seconds): tell the viewer why they should stay. See our guide to TikTok hook examples.
  2. Value: the tip, story or demonstration — cut everything that doesn't serve it.
  3. Ending: a clear finish, such as a summary, a question or a pointer to your next video.

Film vertically, check that your sound is clear, and add on-screen text or captions so the message still works for people watching without sound.

Step 6 — Publish on a schedule you can keep

Consistency beats bursts. A large study by the social media tool Buffer found that accounts posting two to five times a week saw more views per post than accounts posting once a week. Start with a number you can keep for a month — read how often you should post on TikTok for the details.

Tip: batch your work. Plan on one day, film several videos on another, edit and schedule on a third.

Step 7 — Read your numbers after about seven videos

TikTok's analytics live in TikTok Studio (on mobile: your profile → menu → TikTok Studio → Analytics; on desktop: TikTok Studio → Analytics). After your first week, look at three things per video:

MetricWhat it tells you
Average watch timeHow long people stayed — compare it with the video's length.
RetentionWhere people left. A big early drop usually means the opening didn't land.
Shares, saves and commentsWhether the video was useful or interesting enough to act on.

Then change one thing for your next batch — for example only the hook — so you can see what made the difference. Small accounts have small numbers, so treat early results as hints, not proof.

📝 Exercise

Write your one-sentence niche and three pillars. Then list ten video ideas — at least three per pillar — and pick the three easiest to film this week.

Key takeaways

  • The For You feed recommends each video on its own merits; follower count isn't a direct factor.
  • A clear niche and three pillars make ideas easier and your profile clearer.
  • Start with simple videos: one idea, a clear opening, captions.
  • Pick a posting rhythm you can keep — two to five a week is a sensible start.
  • After about seven videos, read your analytics and change one thing at a time.
