Hooks · 7 min read

TikTok hook examples:
7 types that work.

What a hook is, why the first seconds matter, and seven hook types with examples you can adapt — plus a simple way to test which one works for your audience.

By the Creator Signals team · Published 17 November 2026 · Platform facts checked 17 November 2026

The short answer

A hook is the opening line, visual or moment that gives viewers a reason to keep watching. Put your main promise in the first seconds — TikTok's own advertising guidance says to prioritise the hook in the first 6 seconds — and use one clear type: a question, a mistake, a result, curiosity, a bold claim, a how-to or a story. Then test two hooks on the same idea.

What you'll learn

  • What a hook is and why the opening seconds matter
  • Seven hook types, each with examples you can adapt
  • How to test two hooks fairly

What is a hook?

A hook is everything that happens in the first seconds of your video: the first sentence, the first image, the on-screen text and the sound. Its job isn't to be clever — it's to tell one specific viewer, quickly, why this video is for them.

Why the first seconds matter

On the For You feed people decide fast whether to keep watching, and TikTok has said that a viewer watching a longer video to the end is a strong signal in its recommendations. A clear opening is the first step to that.

TikTok's own guidance for advertisers points the same way: its Business Help Center advises prioritising the hook in the first 6 seconds — for example with suspense, surprise or other emotions — and its Creative Codes research (made for ads) reports that most ad-recall impact happens within the first 6 seconds. That research is about ads, not ordinary creator videos, but the lesson carries over: don't save your point for later.

Seven hook types, with examples

The examples below are starting points. Make them specific to your niche — specific beats generic every time.

1. Question

Ask the exact question your viewer is already asking.

  • “Why do your videos stop at 200 views?”
  • “Is it worth batch-cooking for one person?”

2. Mistake

Name a common mistake — viewers stay to check if they make it.

  • “Stop opening your videos with ‘Hi guys’.”
  • “The editing mistake that makes beginners look amateur.”

3. Result first

Show the end result, then explain how you got there.

  • “This took 12 minutes and cost under €4 — here's how.”
  • “Here's my desk before and after one weekend.”

4. Curiosity

Open a loop that only the video can close. Always pay it off.

  • “I tried one change to my captions for a week. Here's what happened.”
  • “There's a setting in your phone camera you've probably never touched.”

5. Bold claim

A clear, defensible opinion. Avoid claims you can't back up.

  • “You don't need a ring light to start.”
  • “Most ‘productivity hacks’ are just good planning.”

6. How-to

Promise a specific skill in a specific time.

  • “How to write a hook in 30 seconds.”
  • “Three steps to clean audio with just your phone.”

7. Story

Start in the middle of a moment.

  • “Two minutes before my first live show, the power went out.”
  • “The comment that changed how I make videos.”

Make the hook work on screen, not just in words

  • Say it and show it. Put the hook in on-screen text as well, so it works with the sound off.
  • Start with movement or a clear image — the finished dish, the before-and-after, your face mid-sentence.
  • Cut the warm-up. No intros, no “so, today…”. Start on the first important word.
  • Keep the promise. If the hook promises three steps, deliver three steps. A hook that misleads may get a view, but people leave early — and that's the signal that matters.

How to test two hooks fairly

  1. Take one video idea and write ten different openings.
  2. Pick the two you like best and film both versions, keeping everything else the same: topic, length, style and roughly the same posting time.
  3. After a few days, compare average watch time and the retention curve in TikTok Studio — especially the first seconds.
  4. Repeat with other videos before drawing conclusions. One test on a small account is a hint, not proof.

📝 Exercise

Choose one video idea. Write one hook for each of the seven types above, then circle the two that feel most natural to say out loud. Need help? Try the free Hook Builder.

Key takeaways

  • A hook is the first line, image, text and sound — together.
  • Put your main promise in the first seconds; TikTok's ad guidance says the first 6.
  • Use one clear type: question, mistake, result, curiosity, bold claim, how-to or story.
  • Show the hook on screen too, so it works without sound.
  • Always deliver what the hook promises — and test two hooks on the same idea.
